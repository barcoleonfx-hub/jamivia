What is PHGG, and why does it dissolve almost clear?

Our largest ingredient starts life as a bean grown in India and Pakistan. Here is what happens to it on the way to your glass.

3 min read · Jamivia Journal

Partially hydrolysed guar gum, PHGG for short, makes up 5 g of every 11.6 g scoop of Jamivia. It is a soluble fibre, and it is the reason the powder is almost invisible in water.

From a bean

Guar gum comes from the seeds of the cluster bean, Cyamopsis tetragonoloba, a drought-hardy crop grown mainly in India and Pakistan. Inside the seed is a long chain of sugars called a galactomannan.

In its natural form those chains are very long, which is why ordinary guar gum is used to thicken sauces and ice cream: a spoonful turns a glass of water to gel.

Gently shortening the chains turns a thickener into a fibre you can stir into water and barely notice.

Shortened, not changed

To make PHGG, the gum is treated with an enzyme that cuts those long chains into shorter pieces. It is still the same fibre, but it no longer thickens. It dissolves almost clear, has very little taste and stays thin in the glass.

The PHGG in Jamivia is 85% fibre, so a scoop provides about 4.2 g of it.

In context

UK guidance is that adults aim for 30 g of fibre a day, from foods such as wholegrains, beans, fruit and vegetables. Most adults eat less than that. A scoop of Jamivia is not a replacement for a varied diet; it is one small, measured part of a day.
